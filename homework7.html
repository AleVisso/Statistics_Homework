Statistical Independence & Contingency Analysis

Theoretical Concepts of Independence in Probability and Statistics

1. Probabilistic Independence

In probability theory, two discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ are independent if and only if their joint probability mass function factors into the product of their individual marginal probability mass functions:

$$ P(X = x_i \text{ and } Y = y_j) = P(X = x_i) \cdot P(Y = y_j) \quad \forall i, j $$

Equivalently, conditional probabilities remain invariant: $P(Y = y_j \mid X = x_i) = P(Y = y_j)$. Knowledge of one variable provides no predictive information regarding the distribution of the other.

2. Statistical Independence in Sample Datasets

In applied statistics, we examine a bivariate sample dataset to test whether categorical attributes exhibit independence. Given observed frequencies $O_{ij}$ in a contingency table with row totals $R_i$ and column totals $C_j$ across total sample size $N$, the expected frequency under the null hypothesis of independence ($H_0$) is defined as:

$$ E_{ij} = \frac{R_i \cdot C_j}{N} $$

The sample counterparts of the probabilities above are the marginal relative frequencies $R_i/N$ and $C_j/N$ and the conditional relative frequencies $O_{ij}/R_i$ (that is $P(Y \mid X)$) and $O_{ij}/C_j$ (that is $P(X \mid Y)$). If the variables are independent, every row of the conditional table is close to the marginal distribution of $Y$.

3. The Pearson Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) Statistic

To measure the divergence between observed and expected frequencies, Karl Pearson formulated the Chi-square statistic:

$$ \chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sum_{j=1}^{c} \frac{(O_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}} $$

Under $H_0$, $\chi^2$ asymptotically follows a Chi-square distribution with degrees of freedom $df = (r - 1)(c - 1)$. Large values of $\chi^2$ lead to the rejection of independence in favor of statistical association. The p-value is $P(\chi^2_{df} \ge \chi^2_{obs})$, and the strength of the association can be summarised by Cramér's $V = \sqrt{\chi^2 / (N \cdot \min(r-1, c-1))}$.
