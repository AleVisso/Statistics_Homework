Theoretical Analysis of Convergence Laws

1. Stochastic Increment Models

Let \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\) be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables representing incremental scores:

  • Bernoulli Model: \(X_i \in \{0, 1\}\) with \(P(X_i = 1) = p\). Expectation \(\mathbb{E}[X_i] = p\), Variance \(\text{Var}(X_i) = p(1-p)\).
  • Rademacher Model: \(X_i \in \{-1, +1\}\) with \(P(X_i = +1) = 1/2\). Expectation \(\mathbb{E}[X_i] = 0\), Variance \(\text{Var}(X_i) = 1\).

2. Behavior of Scaled Aggregates under Different Normalizations

We analyze three distinct scalings of the cumulative process \(S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i\):

Quantity Expression Expected Value \(\mathbb{E}[\cdot]\) Variance \(\text{Var}(\cdot)\) (Rademacher) Asymptotic Behavior (\(n \to \infty\))

Average Score \(S_n / n\) \(\mathbb{E}[X_1]\) \(1/n\) Collapses to Point Mass \(\mu\) (LLN)

Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

According to the Weak Law of Large Numbers, the sample average \(\frac{S_n}{n}\) converges in probability to the theoretical mean \(\mu = \mathbb{E}[X_1]\):

\(\lim_{n \to \infty} P\left(\left| \frac{S_n}{n} - \mu \right| \ge \varepsilon\right) = 0 \quad \forall \varepsilon > 0\)

As \(n\) grows, the variance \(\text{Var}(S_n/n) = \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\) vanishes to zero, forcing trajectory dispersion to contract around \(\mu\).

Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

For the zero-mean Rademacher model (\(\mu = 0, \sigma^2 = 1\)), scaling by \(\sqrt{n}\) stabilizes variance precisely at 1. The normalized quantity converges in distribution to a Standard Gaussian:

\(\frac{S_n - n\mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty\)

This confirms that fluctuations around the mean remain bounded and follow a universal Gaussian profile regardless of discrete step increments.

Cybersecurity Interpretation: Systems, Attackers, and Scale

Physical Meaning of the Model Variables

  • Step Index (\(n\)): Represents system scale, target surface area, or time duration of an attack campaign across \(n\) defense layers/nodes.
  • Trajectory Count (\(m\)): Represents \(m\) distinct threat actors, independent hacking campaigns, or automated botnet instances executing attack vectors concurrently.
  • Bernoulli Model (\(X_i \in \{0, 1\}\)): Models purely cumulative penetration metrics (e.g., total vulnerabilities exploited, compromised hosts, stolen credentials).
  • Rademacher Model (\(X_i \in \{-1, +1\}\)): Models competitive defensive dynamics, where \(+1\) represents successful attacker penetration and \(-1\) represents active defensive remediation, patch deployment, or incident response containment.
Total Score (\(S_n\))

Unscaled cumulative impact. As system scale \(n\) expands, total potential breach exposure grows linearly, and absolute dispersion between attackers widens as \(\sqrt{n}\).

Average Score (\(S_n/n\))

System-wide compromise density per unit node. By the LLN, as enterprise scale \(n \to \infty\), the average compromise rate stabilizes strictly at probability \(p\) (or \(0\) in symmetric defense).

Normalized Score (\(S_n/\sqrt{n}\))

Risk-adjusted threat metric. By normalizing by \(\sqrt{n}\), security teams evaluate attacker proficiency independently of raw system size, comparing performance directly against standard normal confidence bounds.

Practical Defense Takeaways

In large-scale enterprise infrastructure (\(n \gg 1\)), while average vulnerability density stabilizes predictably around expected baselines, total potential damage divergence across many attackers (\(m \gg 1\)) follows a Gaussian envelope. Security architects must size incident response capacity to absorb \(\pm 3\sigma \sqrt{n}\) tail-risk deviations predicted by the Central Limit Theorem.
