Exercise 1: Derivation and Implementation of Welford’s Recurrence Formulas

Welford’s algorithm provides a numerically stable, single-pass method to compute running means and sums of squares without retaining historical data points in memory.

Derivation of the Mean Recurrence Formula

Let \(\mu_{k-1}\) denote the sample mean of the first \(k-1\) observations \(\{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_{k-1}\}\). When a new observation \(x_k\) arrives, the exact definition of the new mean \(\mu_k\) is:

$$ \mu_k = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{i=1}^k x_i = \frac{1}{k} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} x_i + x_k \right) $$

Recognizing that the sum of the first \(k-1\) elements equals \((k-1)\mu_{k-1}\), we substitute and expand:

$$ \mu_k = \frac{1}{k} \left( (k-1)\mu_{k-1} + x_k \right) = \frac{k-1}{k}\mu_{k-1} + \frac{x_k}{k} = \mu_{k-1} + \frac{x_k - \mu_{k-1}}{k} $$

Derivation of the Sum of Squares (SS) Recurrence Formula

Let \(M_2^{(k)} = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \mu_k)^2\) represent the sum of squared deviations from the current mean. Expanding for step \(k\):

$$ M_2^{(k)} = M_2^{(k-1)} + (x_k - \mu_{k-1})(x_k - \mu_k) $$

The sample variance is subsequently derived as \(s^2 = \frac{M_2^{(k)}}{k-1}\).
